# Physical Computing Art: *Boundary Functions*

*Boundary Functions* was an interactive art installation first exhibited at Ars Electronica 1998 in Linz, Austria. Created by Scott Snibbe, the installation consists of a camera and projector connected to a computer, as well as a retroreflective floor. Nothing is projected onto the floor until at least two people have entered the projected area. At that point, lines are projected, dividing the floor into a section around each person. These sections represent the area in which any point is closer to the person in the area than any other person.

These areas are the tangible representation of the relationships and personal space boundaries between people. Personal space can be viewed as an invisible bubble that surrounds everyone, and when someone gets close to you, they push on that bubble. Whether you love or hate someone, this piece shows how we all mold each other's space and our relationships. It is also shown in [this video][installation-video] that when two people touch, their areas combine. I think this represents just how strong some relationships can be. Where two people end up becoming one being, with its own boundaries and relationships.

The areas created by each person combined together is known as a Voronoi diagram. This is a common pattern found throughout all of nature and the world. From the cells in our bodies, to when bubbles touch, to even the placements of chain stores. *Boundary Functions* not only brought people closer in their relationships, but also showed us how our relationships are interconnected with the world around us.
